## 1. Positioning frame — why this is not a generic wanderlust brand

TruTravel is a **technocratic travel-community product**, not a lifestyle-inspiration brand.
Shared Context §4 defines the technocratic UI posture as: systems over vibes-only chaos, trust
surfaces first-class, data density without clutter, explicit states. Voice has to carry the
same posture in words, or the brand fractures the moment a user reads copy next to a screen.

Translating each UI principle into a voice rule:

| Technocratic UI principle | Voice translation |
|---|---|
| Systems over vibes-only chaos | Copy is structured and specific. Mood language is allowed but never *replaces* facts — every emotive line sits next to a concrete one (dates, group size, price band, verification). |
| Trust surfaces first-class | Verification, group composition, and policy are never buried in fine print or a separate "safety" page. They are load-bearing copy, foregrounded at the same moment as the sell. |
| Community instrumental to booking, not a separate social network | Content always resolves toward a bookable state (a departure, a seat, a captain, a corridor). It is not engagement-for-its-own-sake. No content ritual exists purely to farm likes/shares/DMs. |
| Data density without clutter | Facts are stated plainly and once, not padded with repeated adjectives. One well-chosen number beats three superlatives. |
| Explicit states | No ambiguous CTAs, no vague urgency. State things as they are ("6 of 12 seats filled," "verified Anchor Operator," "guide certified: Grade III") rather than implying them ("almost full!", "trusted", "epic"). |
| Home surfaces all six archetypes with equal structural weight | No segment's voice is "the real TruTravel voice" with others as reskins. All six get equal craft investment — see §2. |

The single biggest failure mode to design against: **defaulting to a generic "wanderlust"
voice** — breathless, adjective-stacked, interchangeable across any travel brand. If a sentence
could be dropped into a generic OTA's Instagram caption unchanged, it is not on-brand.

## 2. One brand, six dialects — how voice flexes per segment

TruTravel is **one brand speaking six dialects**, not six sub-brands and not one flattened
voice stretched over six product lines. Two failure modes to avoid equally:

- **Over-flattening**: applying one "TruTravel voice" (typically inflected toward Trippy/
  wanderlust) across all six segments — this is what erases Thrilling's competence-register,
  makes CodeHouses sound like a yoga retreat, and is the direct cause of Couples-as-dating-app
  and Trippy-as-drug-marketplace failure modes (segments borrow inappropriate registers from
  each other by default).
- **Over-fracturing**: treating each segment as an unrelated brand with no shared grammar — a
  user who has booked a Thrilling trip should still recognize TruTravel's hand in Wellness copy.

**The fix: a fixed brand grammar (constant across all six) + a flexible register layer
(vocabulary, tone dial, sentence rhythm — varies per segment).**

### 2.1 Brand grammar — constant across every segment

These rules apply to every piece of TruTravel content regardless of segment:

1. **Every persuasive claim sits next to a verifiable fact.** "Loved by our community" is not
   allowed alone; "4.8/5 across 340 verified travelers" is.
2. **No manufactured urgency.** No countdown-bait, no "only X left!!" unless X is a real,
   currently-true number pulled from live state. State the real number; let scarcity speak for
   itself if it exists.
3. **Second person, direct address.** "You're joining a group of 8" beats "We invite you to
   join our community" — TruTravel talks *to* the traveler, not *about* itself.
4. **CTAs are verb + concrete object.** "Reserve your seat," "Meet your captain," "See the
   route" — never bare "Explore" or "Discover" with no object.
5. **Superlatives require evidence.** "Best," "ultimate," "epic" are banned unless the next six
   words substantiate them with a fact or attributed quote.
6. **Minimal emoji, never as a punctuation crutch.** A segment may use emoji sparingly where it
   matches genuine register (e.g., a festival lineup drop) — never to disguise a vague sentence.
7. **Trust information is never optional flavor text.** Operator verification status, captain
   identity, and safety/policy basics appear in the same content unit as the pitch, not a
   separate page a user has to go find.
8. **Community content always resolves to a bookable object** — a trip, a seat, a corridor, a
   captain profile with a next departure. No orphan "vibes" content with no path to booking.

### 2.2 Register layer — how it flexes per segment

Each segment gets its own **tone dial** (how warm/crisp/calm/etc.) and **vocabulary bank**,
governed by the shared grammar above. See segment cards in §3.

## 3. Segment voice cards

Each card: tone, vocabulary to use, words/registers to avoid, and the non-negotiable redline.
These are principles-level — final copywriting per corridor is downstream execution work.

### 3.1 Trippy Tours — free-spirited, conscious group travel

- **Tone dial:** warm, present-tense, sensory-but-grounded. Reads like a thoughtful friend
  describing a shared plan, not a festival flyer.
- **Vocabulary:** circle, gathering, shared journey, present, open, slow travel, grounded,
  unplugged, small group, shared intention.
- **Avoid:** any word that reads as a substance wink ("high," "blaze," "trip" used as a pun,
  "lost weekend," "no rules"), "chill vibes only" used as a *substitute* for actual policy
  information, imagery/language implying unsupervised or substance-driven experiences.
- **Redline (non-negotiable):** Trippy content must **never read as drug-marketplace-adjacent**.
  Every "free-spirited" claim is paired with concrete trust facts in the same unit — captain
  name, code of conduct, verified group composition. Vibe language never stands alone.

### 3.2 Thrilling Tours — adventure, adrenaline, skill-challenge

- **Tone dial:** crisp, competent, confident without bravado. Adrenaline framed as capability,
  not recklessness.
- **Vocabulary:** route, difficulty grade, briefing, certified guide, gear list, summit,
  descent, skill level, conditions.
- **Avoid:** "extreme," "insane," "no fear," "YOLO," hype-only adjectives with no competence
  signal attached.
- **Redline:** Every thrill claim is paired with a safety/skill fact in the same breath —
  difficulty grade, guide certification, or gear checklist. No adventure-porn copy without a
  competence signal next to it.

### 3.3 Wellness Tours — yoga, recovery, nature reset

- **Tone dial:** calm, unhurried, specific rather than vaguely mystical. Practitioners are named
  and credentialed, not mythologized.
- **Vocabulary:** practice, reset, guided, restorative, facilitator, integration, pace, quiet.
- **Avoid:** medical/therapeutic claims ("heals trauma," "cures burnout"), spiritual-bypass
  language, "detox" or "cleanse" without a factual basis, "guru" unless it is someone's actual
  earned title.
- **Redline:** Wellness content **never makes medical or therapeutic promises**. Every claim
  about a practice is attributable to a named, credentialed facilitator.

### 3.4 Couple Getaways — dual-traveler, privacy-first

- **Tone dial:** private, considerate, low-pressure, practical about logistics (room
  configuration, pacing, privacy controls). Warm but never flirtatious *about the platform's
  role* — the platform is a logistics/privacy enabler, not a third party in the relationship.
- **Vocabulary:** the two of you, your pace, privacy-first, quiet corners, just for you two.
- **Avoid:** "match," "spark," "chemistry," "swipe," "singles," "meet someone," any language
  implying the platform helps a user find or vet a romantic partner.
- **Redline (non-negotiable):** Couple Getaways content must **never read as a dating app**.
  Copy always assumes the couple is already formed. No partner-discovery language, no framing
  of other travelers as potential matches, no public social feed of couples' activity —
  privacy-first means this segment gets the *least* public community-ritual content of the six
  (see §4.6).

### 3.5 CodeHouses — builder/founder co-living workations

- **Tone dial:** efficient, peer-to-peer, low-hype — reads like a well-written README, not a
  "digital nomad paradise" ad.
- **Vocabulary:** workation, deep work block, cowork, output, sprint, stack, shipped, quiet
  hours, desk setup.
- **Avoid:** "digital nomad paradise," excessive "vibes"-first framing, hustle-culture bravado
  with no substance behind it.
- **Redline:** CodeHouses content always states concrete work infrastructure (connectivity,
  desk/workspace setup, quiet-hours policy) in the same unit as any lifestyle/aesthetic framing.
  Never sell purely on aesthetic without infra facts.

### 3.6 Music + Art Festivals — festival/art-circuit crew travel

- **Tone dial:** energetic but organized — excitement is grounded in logistics (lineup, pass
  tier, transport, gate time), not manufactured hype.
- **Vocabulary:** lineup, crew, pass tier, gate time, stage, circuit, transport window.
- **Avoid:** FOMO-manipulation ("sold out forever," fake countdowns), and — critically —
  any language that implies TruTravel directly sells/controls festival tickets when fulfillment
  is actually via affiliate/pass partner (Shared Context §2.6, §3).
- **Redline:** Content must always be accurate about **what is booked directly on TruTravel
  (travel + stay) vs. fulfilled via affiliate/pass partner (the festival ticket itself)**. Never
  obscure this mechanic to make the offer look more complete than it is.

### 3.7 Cross-segment contrast check (quick sanity test)

Before shipping any piece of content, ask: *if I swapped this copy into a different segment's
page, would it still make sense unedited?* If yes, it is too generic — it is "wanderlust
voice," not segment-native voice, and should be rewritten with that segment's vocabulary bank
and redline in mind.

## 4. Content ritual principles

"Ritual" content is **recurring, cadence-bound content tied to the community/booking rhythm** —
not one-off campaign posts and not vanity-metric content calendars. Full weekly-runnable
playbooks (specific formats, channels, copy templates) are downstream execution work for
`docs/ops/content/`; this section locks the *principles* those playbooks must obey.

### 4.1 Principle: rituals loop back to trust or booking, never to vanity alone

Every recurring content format must have an explicit answer to "what bookable or trust-building
state does this reinforce?" Likes/shares/follows are acceptable *byproducts*, never the design
goal of a ritual. This is the content-layer expression of "community is instrumental to
booking, not a separate social network" (Shared Context §4).

### 4.2 Principle: cadence follows the booking/departure calendar, not a generic content calendar

Ritual timing is anchored to real product rhythm — corridor departure dates, pre-trip windows,
post-trip windows, seasonal corridor cycles — not an arbitrary "post 3x/week" schedule
disconnected from what's actually happening in the marketplace. This also keeps India-corridor
depth (Shared Context §5) reflected in content: rituals should feel native to a specific
corridor's calendar, not globally generic.

### 4.3 Principle: every ritual has an explicit success state

Per the "explicit states" UI principle, each ritual format must define, up front, what "working"
looks like in observable terms (e.g., "captain-verification post → seat reservations within 72h"
not "post → engagement"). Ambiguous vanity engagement is never the stated success metric.

### 4.4 Principle: ritual families, not one-off content

Three ritual *families* recur across segments (execution playbooks will instantiate these per
segment/corridor):

1. **Proof-of-trust ritual** — recurring surfacing of who is running/hosting a trip (Anchor
   Operator credentials, Captain identity, safety/policy basics). This is the content-layer
   twin of "trust surfaces first-class."
2. **Group-composition preview ritual** — pre-departure content showing an aggregated,
   privacy-respecting preview of who's coming (headcount, broad composition), reinforcing that
   this is a real group forming, not a content feed pretending to be one.
3. **Post-trip evidence ritual** — recurring recap content (facts + story) that proves a past
   trip delivered on its promise, feeding the trust loop for the next cohort's booking decision.

### 4.5 Principle: ritual format is segment-native, not copy-pasted across segments

The *family* (§4.4) is shared; the *format* must be segment-native, following each segment's
tone dial and redlines from §3:

| Segment | Native ritual format flavor |
|---|---|
| Trippy | Circle reflections — small, grounded recap of a group's shared experience, always paired with captain/trust facts (never a standalone "vibes" post). |
| Thrilling | Route/skill logs — factual recap of route completed, conditions, skill level required — adventure-as-evidence, not adventure-as-hype. |
| Wellness | Practice journals — facilitator-attributed recap of a practice/session, no therapeutic-outcome claims. |
| Couple Getaways | **Minimal-to-no public ritual content.** Privacy-first means this segment intentionally gets the lightest public community-content footprint of the six — see §4.6. |
| CodeHouses | Build/output logs — what got shipped during the stay, infra facts, peer-to-peer tone (README-like, not lifestyle-brand-like). |
| Music + Art Festivals | Lineup/crew countdown — grounded in real logistics (transport window, gate time, pass-tier clarity), not fabricated urgency. |

### 4.6 Principle: privacy floor for Couple Getaways is a ritual-design constraint, not an afterthought

Because the redline for this segment is "never read as a dating app," the ritual system for
Couples must default to **less public content, not more** — no public recap of "who a couple
was," no aggregated-but-still-identifying group previews. Where a ritual family (§4.4) doesn't
have a safe, non-dating-app-coded expression for Couples, the correct answer is to skip it for
that segment rather than force-fit it.

### 4.7 Principle: India-corridor depth over global sprawl in ritual cadence

Per working agreement in Shared Context §5, ritual playbooks should be built corridor-first
(one corridor × one segment proven before expanding) rather than a single global content
calendar retrofitted across corridors. This is a scope note for the future
`docs/ops/content/` playbooks, not something this principles doc itself needs to resolve.

## 5. Non-negotiables (redlines) — consolidated

1. **Trippy Tours never reads as a drug marketplace.** Vibe/freedom language is always paired
   with concrete trust facts in the same content unit.
2. **Couple Getaways never reads as a dating app.** No partner-discovery language; copy always
   assumes an already-formed couple; ritual content footprint is intentionally minimal (§4.6).
3. **No superlative or urgency claim without evidence.** "Best," "almost full," "ultimate" are
   banned unless immediately substantiated with a real, current fact.
4. **Every trust claim must be UI-renderable, not just adjectival.** "Verified," "certified,"
   "safe" must correspond to a fact the product can actually surface (operator license,
   guide certification, policy doc) — content should not promise trust the product can't show.
5. **No content ritual exists purely for engagement.** Every recurring format must trace to a
   trust-building or bookable outcome (§4.1).
6. **Shared brand grammar (§2.1) applies to all six segments without exception**, even as
   vocabulary and tone dial flex per segment.
7. **Festival-segment content is always accurate about direct-booking vs. affiliate/pass
   fulfillment.** Never blur what TruTravel books directly vs. what a partner fulfills.
8. **Wellness content never makes medical/therapeutic claims;** claims are always attributable
   to a named, credentialed facilitator.
9. **No segment voice defaults to "generic wanderlust."** Use the cross-segment contrast check
   (§3.7) before shipping.
