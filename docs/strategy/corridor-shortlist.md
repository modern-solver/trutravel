## 3. Demand hypotheses (top 3 corridors)

### Himachal Adventure Belt × Thrilling Tours
1. Skill/pace-based matching (trek grade, altitude experience, gear readiness) is a strong
   wedge for solo trekkers who currently self-assemble groups via WhatsApp/Instagram — **med
   confidence** (inferred from goSTOPS CARAVAN and Zostel community features already
   patching this gap informally; no primary user interview yet).
2. India adventure tourism market was ~**$16.7B in 2024, projected ~$86B by 2033 (17.8%
   CAGR)** **[estimate, IMARC via search aggregation]** — directionally strong but treat the
   terminal number skeptically; CAGRs this high over 8+ years are rarely realized cleanly —
   **med confidence** on direction, **low confidence** on magnitude.
3. Corridor already has anchor-operator-ready supply (Indiahikes, Bikat Adventures) and
   distribution infra (Zostel/goStops hostel density) — lowers cold-start cost vs. a
   greenfield corridor — **high confidence**.
4. Kasol/Parvati Valley's countercultural reputation gives Trippy Tours a natural adjacent
   expansion inside the same corridor, but carries real legal/brand risk (regional cannabis
   association) that must be firewalled per the "not a drug marketplace" rule — **flag for
   A4, high urgency**.

### Rishikesh–Uttarakhand × Wellness Tours
1. India wellness tourism market is **$27.92B (2025) → $38.22B (2030), ~6.48% CAGR**, with
   North India (Rishikesh/Haridwar circuit) the fastest-growing sub-region at **~18.54%
   CAGR through 2031**, per Mordor Intelligence-sourced aggregation — **med confidence**
   (aggregated third-party estimate, not verified against primary Mordor report).
2. Wellness travelers plausibly place higher value on match quality (pace, diet, silence
   vs. social retreat style) than price — good fit for a trust-first, non-race-to-the-bottom
   platform — **low confidence** (hypothesis, no primary survey data yet).
3. Year-round demand (yoga teacher training cycles run continuously, unlike trekking's
   narrow Apr–Jun/Sep–Nov windows) could reduce Anchor Operator idle-capacity risk — **med
   confidence**, should be validated by A2 against real occupancy data.
4. Lowest legal/safety exposure of the three shortlisted segments (no drug adjacency, no
   crowd-safety/ticketing fraud exposure, no dating-app-perception risk) — best aligns with
   "when uncertain between growth and trust, choose trust" — **high confidence** as a
   qualitative read, not a market-size claim.

### Goa × Music+Art Festivals
1. Sunburn (Vagator, Dec) is India's largest EDM festival and a proven high-intent travel
   trigger — **high confidence** demand exists, but it is extremely seasonal (Dec–Jan
   concentration), which stresses the "healthy contribution margin, not race to bottom"
   model if TruTravel can't fill off-season capacity — **med confidence** on the risk.
2. Goa recorded ~1.08 crore total tourist arrivals in 2025 (domestic + foreign), continuing
   sustained post-COVID growth — **high confidence** [Goa Dept. of Tourism, 2025 figures].
3. This corridor is the most competitively saturated of the shortlist — affiliate ticket
   resellers, established DMCs, and Zo World's Goa footprint all compete for the same
   festival-travel spend, likely compressing take rate below the 18–25% target — **med
   confidence** risk flag.

## 4. Top recommendation (for founder decision — not a unilateral pick)

**Recommended first vertical slice: Himachal Adventure Belt (Manali–Kasol–Tosh–Bir corridor)
× Thrilling Tours.**

Rationale:
- Best corridor-depth story in the shortlist: the same geography can plausibly extend to
  Trippy Tours (Kasol/Parvati culture) and CodeHouses (Dharamkot/Bir coworking, where
  NomadGao-style supply already exists) as segments #2 and #3 without leaving the corridor —
  directly satisfies the "prefer India corridor depth over shallow global sprawl" working
  agreement.
- Lowest cold-start supply risk: Indiahikes and Bikat Adventures are plausible Anchor
  Operator candidates already operating at scale in-corridor; Zostel/goStops hostel density
  gives an existing Captain-recruitment and distribution surface.
- Clearest technical showcase for the matching engine (P3's mandate): trek-grade, pace, and
  gear-readiness matching is a concrete, testable v0 scoring problem, more tractable than
  Couple Getaways' privacy-sensitive matching or Music+Art's ticket-inventory complexity.

Two things the founder should weigh before locking this in:
1. **WanderOn is explicitly moving into "adventure travel, sports-led trips, and wellness
   tourism"** with fresh Series A capital — this is the single most direct near-term
   competitive collision in this shortlist and should factor into timing urgency.
2. **Kasol/Parvati Valley's cannabis-culture adjacency is a real brand/legal risk** if Trippy
   Tours expands into this corridor next — needs an A4 review before any Trippy Tours
   content or partner is greenlit here.

**Runner-up worth keeping live:** Rishikesh–Uttarakhand × Wellness Tours — meaningfully
lower legal/safety exposure, best alignment with the "choose trust" tiebreaker rule, and the
fastest-growing sub-market by CAGR in this research pass. If the founder weighs trust/safety
above speed-to-market and competitive collision risk, this is the stronger pick.

Both are live options; this is a recommendation for founder sign-off, not a locked decision.

   Rishikesh-Wellness given operator margins in each (Bikat/Indiahikes-style trekking margins
   vs. yoga-retreat margins)? Does off-season idle capacity kill the 18–25% contribution
   target in either corridor?

   Operators running high-altitude treks (Himachal) vs. yoga/wellness retreats (Rishikesh)?
   What's the concrete legal exposure of a "Trippy Tours" presence in Kasol/Parvati Valley?
3. **A3 (Segment Insight):** Do solo trekkers/wellness travelers actually self-report
   dissatisfaction with ad hoc WhatsApp/Instagram group formation today? (This doc's demand
   hypotheses are largely inferred from competitor UX signals, not primary user interviews.)
4. **A1 (follow-up):** Primary-source verification of the IMARC $86B-by-2033 adventure
   tourism figure and the Mordor Intelligence 18.54% North India wellness CAGR — both were
   pulled via aggregated search summaries, not the original reports.
5. **G2 (Supply Ops):** Would Indiahikes/Bikat Adventures (Himachal) or established Rishikesh
   ashram/retreat operators actually engage as Anchor Operators under TruTravel's take-rate
   model, or do they see themselves as end-to-end competitors rather than supply partners?
6. **P1/P3:** Which segment's matching problem (trek-grade/pace vs. wellness-style/pace vs.
